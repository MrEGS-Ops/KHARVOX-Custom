# Object Activation Investigator (experimental)

DEV > Grapple Grab > Activation Investigator. Session-only, defaults OFF. Enabling it automatically enables the discovery scanner and cyan laser, suspends all grapple/automatic melee and prevents native grab acquisition. Other saved preferences are preserved. Point the weapon-hand ray (right by default, left if weapon handedness is swapped) at a stationary grey barrel for a moment, then perform ordinary melee with that same weapon hand, kick, or run a control trial with no attack. The cyan investigator laser follows the weapon hand; the free-hand grab ray resumes after disabling the investigator. Keep the object in the loaded level for 30 seconds. Repeat with fresh props and miss trials. Disable the investigator to resume ordinary grabbing.

[ACTIVATION-INV] logs include a signature-checked candidate wake function RVA 0x179A8F0 (machine code writes 0xffffffff at body +0x170), matching virtual slots, native velocity offsets +0x1EC and +0x1F8, pose, timestamped body/entity word changes and XOR masks (first 0x300 bytes each), native melee input edges, and observed displacement. The target is an estimated ray candidate, not verified melee contact. Once an event is detected, the identity is latched for 30 seconds; reads are 12ms initially and 100ms after two seconds, with generation/identity/read guards. Natural sleep may take longer. Compare transitions, idle controls, misses and repeated trials. Logs do not prove exact within-tick ordering, actual execution of the candidate function, or that the wake word is an active flag. No unverified functions are called, no hooks are installed, and no physics state is written by the investigator. Direct call-path tracing is a separate next step after validating the candidate with headset evidence.
